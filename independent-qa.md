# Independent research and MVP checks

Research snapshot: 26 October 2026. Three research agents separately investigated candidates and challenged one another’s legal mappings, currentness, dates and conclusions. The coordinating researcher checked disputed primary texts and integrated accepted records. These are AI-assisted research checks, not expert legal opinions.

## Accepted catalogue

56 records across 22 Member States and 28 EU changes. The second pass added 19 records to the original ten; the third added 16; the completed fourth added eleven, including two without exact prior recognition located. That group now contains 19 candidates; the other 37 concerns were already identified by an authority or published analysis. Recognition is separate from legal strength. Fully resolved cases are excluded from public data, counts and exports. This is a dated snapshot, not live compliance monitoring. Earlier records keep their original evidence dates; this pass did not revalidate every earlier concern as of 26 October.

## Material adverse checks from the fourth pass

- **Hungary, bank capital:** the authentic August correction and current October national Section 186(9)–(12) were independently read. The all-Tier-1 trigger still selects non-leverage risks rather than excessive leverage. Supervisory discretion, EU-consistent interpretation and the distinct directly applicable CRR baseline remain explicit. A public issuer disclosure concerning ordinary additional-capital composition did not establish actual leverage-related supervisory effects; no bank inadequacy is alleged. A final check confirmed CRD VI leaves Article 104a(4) unchanged; current subsection (8) concerns calculation amounts rather than supplying the missing composition rule. Bounded exact-issue searches found no prior recognition of the national mismatch.
- **Romania, group-investor exception:** all three researchers independently read the current national body with April 2026 amendment notes and the October authentic EU correction. Older historical portal and regulator copies were rejected as currentness evidence. Branches and subsidiaries are distinct categories in the same statute. Lawful independent national regulation of entities outside the directive, holding-company exclusions and interpretation remain explicit; no compulsory national exemption, unlawful authorisation or affected manager was asserted. Bounded searches located no precise prior acknowledgment.
- **Belgium and Sweden, single permits:** both current statutes and administrative pages retain shorter periods; the six-month EU protection concerns qualifying holders with more than two years’ tenure, remaining validity and applicable conditions. Belgium’s rule runs from loss of work authorisation, not necessarily the dismissal date. Separate Blue Card regimes, EU-derived individual rights and other residence grounds were challenged. Sweden’s February 2027 change remains a proposal. The Netherlands was excluded because its current immigration guidance already grants the longer period.
- **Poland, environmental crime:** current October Water Law and Penal Code consolidations were compared with the new abstraction offence and the official UC148 proposal. Existing permit offences, actual-damage crimes, attempt and endangerment were adverse checks. The remaining concern is risk-only abstraction, particularly serious negligence; the EU three-year maximum requirement is limited to intentional conduct. This is a replacement directive, not a case causally established from the separate Polish corrigendum.
- **Germany, trafficking services:** the customer offence is still sexual-services-specific. Exploitation, complicity and knowing employment offences have additional elements; some cases remain criminal already. The official broader-customer proposal and September hearing recognize the coverage issue. No failed prosecution or universal customer immunity was claimed.
- **Netherlands, anti-SLAPP:** the live current jurisdiction rules, July implementation act and commencement, withdrawn proposed jurisdiction amendment, court discussion and contrary government sufficiency position were checked. Existing fallback jurisdiction and EU-derived rights remain qualified. The cited court accepted jurisdiction under existing law and did not find a current EU breach. The separate costs issue was corrected in July and excluded.
- **Portugal and Czechia, consumer amendments:** the Portuguese live consolidated withdrawal provision remains conditional on a trader offering the electronic route. Existing withdrawal rights and voluntary functions are preserved. Czechia’s authentic new human-explanation provision starts only in January 2027; current Section 1845 concerns contract terms and communications. Sectoral explanation regimes, GDPR safeguards, voluntary support and conforming interpretation prevent a universal customer-assistance claim. Both concerns have prior recognition.
- **Portugal, repair extension:** the existing three-year baseline and repeated six-month increments were tested against the authentic twelve-month incentive and the longer-period option. The special no-fixed-liability alternative does not obviously apply. Exact published analysis already identifies the issue; the national consumer authority describes the new measure. Both repair records focus conservatively on contracts after 31 July; this does not assert a 1 August legal commencement. The original act, official amendment register and current prosecutorial legal portal were read. No refused claim or generally inferior Portuguese warranty was alleged.
- **France, electricity contracts:** the live code through 26 October ends the relevant chapter at L332-7 and requires dynamic offers. The existing one-year consumer option is a duration requirement, not a fixed-price obligation. The proposed additional entitlement remains in the parliamentary dossier; commercial products and voluntary commitments were checked and remain explicit.
- **Fresh correction controls:** Dutch RED Article 29(10) date references already use the corrected boundary or incorporate the EU criteria; a stale annual-report footnote did not prove an operative gap. The May Dutch procurement correction adds a conjunction in a recital. Spain’s flexible-work request protection was restored in April 2025, before the March 2026 EU correction. The May French minimum-tax corrections concern grammar and equivalent permanent-establishment terminology. None was promoted to a public finding. Further controls checked French drinking-water information sharing and accessibility, Polish groundwater terminology, Danish and Slovak waste corrections, Austrian tax exceptions, and asylum-school access. Explicit incorporation, equivalent current duties or lawful alternative routes prevented unsupported additions. A structured official index of the 100 newest directive corrigenda helped prioritize this screen; it was not treated as evidence of national noncompliance.

A final Finnish VAT control found that current national business-transfer rules and earlier tax guidance already supply the corrected whole-business or autonomous-part meaning. The Swedish bank-resolution terminology correction did not itself establish a substantive national omission. Both were kept outside the public catalogue.

The second pass concentrated on repeatedly revised frameworks: renewable energy, energy efficiency, workplace exposure, financial markets and payment services, tax cooperation, building performance, toys and restricted substances. It followed changed provisions into dated national rules, rather than treating a missing transposition notification as sufficient proof.

## Material adverse checks from the second pass

- **France, diisocyanates:** the EU binding table and base directive’s national-binding-limit obligation were checked against both French tables. The interim figures convert correctly but appear only as indicative prevention objectives; the lower binding values start in 2029. Two agents independently challenged that legal distinction. A third read the live current code on 25 October, corrected a stale article identifier and confirmed the deferred row. General prevention, monitoring and inspection powers remain qualified. Public guidance describes the staging; no exact acknowledgment of its potential incompatibility was located.
- **Ireland, biomass:** the statutory 20 MW threshold was checked against the revised 7.5 MW criterion, the amendment register and later-measure searches. The record concerns renewable-energy accounting, obligations and support, not a general ban on burning biomass. Old fixed long-term support, separate scheme conditions and EU ETS protections remain qualifications.
- **Poland, biomass:** the August consolidated threshold and original-act amendment register were checked against the signed September act published in October. That enactment does not amend Article 135a. A separate government project proposes the lower threshold, so prior recognition is marked. The record is limited to newer installations outside qualifying old support, with no individual grant or accounting decision audited.
- **Italy, biomass:** the new 7.5 MW threshold is already present. The concern is a temporary automatic-compliance policy without the new verification, rather than an unchanged threshold. The old August 2024 certification decree was read and distinguished from the later simplified scheme still described as pending by the ministry in May 2026. The EU allowance for simplified checks and grandfathered support is retained.
- **Ireland, residential charging:** agents checked the recast’s new-building requirements against current Part L2A and later building instruments. Empty ducts differ from pre-cabling and an installed charger. National planning guidance and an example of stricter local conditions were checked; local requirements and the absence of an actual building audit are disclosed.
- **Belgium and Luxembourg, DAC8:** the exact French correction was compared with the newly enacted address references. Belgian Community/Regional tax competence is distinguished from the whole national regime. Later Luxembourg measures concern filing mechanics and do not fix the reference. Direct EU definitions, administrative interpretation and the first reporting timetable limit practical claims.
- **France, Denmark and Latvia, PSD2:** agents traced the corrected non-consumer opt-out article into each national provision. Later amendments and official legislative mappings were checked. This is a contractual-choice question, not missing consumer refund protection. Full harmonisation, conforming interpretation and other contractual routes remain open.
- **Slovenia, RoHS:** independent review caught the direction of the exception to a transport exclusion. The stale wording can bring qualifying non-electric two-wheel EEE within national restrictions; it does not remove electric vehicles from protection. The EEE definition and later amendment were checked, with no particular product or enforcement effect established.
- **Italy, lead; Cyprus, asbestos; Hungary, cobalt:** exact limits or permitted uses were compared with the revised EU provisions, current tables, later registers and official pending remedies. Lead’s earlier-exposure exceptions, asbestos’s separate 2029 measurement rules and toys’ other exceptions are preserved. These concerns are officially recognised.
- **Spain, energy audits; Germany, utilities procurement:** changed duties were compared with the current SME exclusion and discretionary energy award criteria respectively. Regional duties, existing information requirements, federal-only instructions and individual tender conditions were considered. Draft remedies are distinguished from enacted rules.
- **France, financial markets:** the issuer-value cap and systematic-internaliser definition remain in the current code. The AMF’s joint-payment position and ESMA’s relief from quantitative testing are expressly retained, so stale text is not equated with a continuing practical prohibition.
- **Spain, payment-system access:** both the institution definition and designated-system exemption were read against the EU changes. TARGET already permits discretionary non-bank admission and existing law protects indirect access. Those safeguards prevent a blanket-access-ban claim. The July bill is not enacted law; October dissolution was checked against the official decree.

## Earlier pilot checks retained

The original ten records were independently checked against current national texts and the exact EU change. Important limits remain visible: lower electromagnetic-exposure limits can be lawful stricter protection; Danish guidance directs conforming application; waste terminology alone does not prove weaker protection; posted-worker rights may arise elsewhere; and an old court judgment describes its legally relevant assessment date. ## Candidates excluded after challenge

Potential cases were held or rejected where another legal route could supply the duty, a purported amendment only changed a procedure, a later correction had already resolved the concern, or the currentness check was incomplete. Examples included general counterparty-risk plans, alternative producer-responsibility and criminal-sanction routes, and administrative energy-certificate requirements. Additional third-pass controls excluded Portuguese motor-insurance wording where broader national scope is permitted, corrected Spanish dual-quality wording, and Maltese/Cypriot/Greek DAC8 alternatives that may already supply the corrected meaning. A final joint screen also held the April 2026 Slovak REDIII terminology correction and Polish environmental-crime range correction: no substantive current national omission was established. These screening controls are not listed as public findings.

## Source and file policy

The public dataset keeps concise paraphrases, exact identifiers, dates, source URLs and pinpoints; published commentary is used for recognition, while official legal sources support comparisons. No full evidence PDFs or source archives are bundled. Repeated prose across shared EU acts was shortened after a source-budget audit. Research drafts and temporary source material remain outside the project; only final concise records are packaged here. A final recognition sweep also revisited the earlier French diisocyanate and Irish residential-charging leads. Published descriptions of national staging or ducting were distinguished from exact recognition of the compared EU mismatch. No claim of first discovery is made.
